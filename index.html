Describe your tool

1. What are you building it in?

How much shifts to the vendor depends enormously on which one. A hosted builder like ArcGIS StoryMaps settles about half the technical criteria for you. A library or notebook — Leaflet, Jupyter, R Shiny, Observable — leaves nearly all of them yours, because you are the one writing the interface. Your answer doesn't shorten the list below; it labels who owns each item, and "shared" still means you are the one who verifies it.

2. What kind of tool is it? Select all that apply.

Many tools are several at once — a dashboard with an embedded map, or a scrollytelling story built around charts. This pre-selects the feature modules most likely to be relevant; you can change any of them next.

3. Which version of WCAG are you working to?

WCAG 2.2 adds four criteria, and all four bear directly on interactive data tools. Leave this on unless the policy you answer to still names 2.1 — some procurement rules and national regulations have not adopted 2.2 yet.

Your answers above switch on the features most likely to be relevant; correct them here. Each feature you confirm unlocks a small module of criteria, and everything you leave off stays out of your list.

    What this list does and doesn't do

    This tool scopes which criteria apply to your tool. It does not certify conformance. Conformance still requires human testing — a keyboard walkthrough and a screen-reader pass — because automated scanners catch only about a third of issues, and the hardest parts of interactive data tools (focus management, meaningful alternatives, drag alternatives) are exactly the parts a scanner cannot judge.
